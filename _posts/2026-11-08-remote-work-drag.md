You open your email and the title is clear: **"Congratulations! An offer from Acme, Inc. has been extended."**

Celebration and awe. You were not part of these people before, you were not even in the same conversation where remote was put in place.

Your six to seven hours spent per week in the subway made you resilient but unaware: there was another world out there.

You walked around the streets, you passed through a few parks, and you observed: people playing tennis at 10:00AM, a few having breakfast while on a business call, others reading a book while another device of theirs played a boring, monotone conversation with their next in line.

There must be something magical about working from home. Waking up in the morning close to your desk, moving a few meters from your spot towards the desk where you will spend the next few hours ahead. The expectation of not being in traffic, nor on public transportation.

Your soul in place. Your mind in the game, again.

The very first day, you receive a laptop, you configure it and start chatting, you go ahead, you are being "onboarded" to a set of "rooms" and "chats" where everyone hangs out and wanders around. There's no chit-chat. You are afraid of sending the first message.

You wait.

You wait...

A person from the team reaches out and asks: "Hey, did you chat already with our PM?"

Did I what?, you think, it's my first day. I don't even know what we do.

But you don't say much.

Days pass by.

You start every morning, from the very first days, and you start to realize: almost everything looks exactly the same, and you move on, from every month to every year. The discussions. The meetings. The breaks. A [copy of a copy of a copy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copy_of_A).

But you are not confused, you are just dazed by the fact that you are wrong. You cannot be the only one hating this. You believe something else might be there for you to do. A coffee at 10AM, why not? I work remotely! I can bring my laptop! So you do... and you ask for a coffee, only to realize there's no space, no Wi-Fi, no power outlet to connect to. So you keep going. You keep the mindset: it must be something I'm doing wrong.

You change your routine, and now instead of waking up plus work, you do waking up, gym, and a bit of a walk before you start, only to realize your work hours might be overlapping with people in other timezones, so you sneak a bit on Slack.

[24 new messages, while you slept](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/worklab/work-trend-index/breaking-down-infinite-workday).

Well, gym might wait, this is work, it's important so I must

You open, you check the clock, almost 11AM, nothing more than sitting at a desk in a room close to the same death bed you just woke up from.

You sneak a bit outside and you go out, time for a walk... but your instinct makes you keep your phone in your pocket. So you read. You listen. You jump to issues and wonder why this individual does not consider you in the problem you both worked on together.

Your mind starts to overcomplicate a lot of these things, ending up in the realization that you don't really know this person you are actually complaining about.

Days go by, months now... and you think of all the people that now are lost in the network, that you don't have contact with. The grinds and hours you did for solely nothing. The bytes you are and the information you represent.

Remote work. Of course it's work, but it's remote! Meaning anyone, anywhere, at any time, can reach out to you. Can ask for your opinion and wait for you to be online again, to just ask you the same question twice.

The collaboration is now a prison.

An infinite work day, once again.

The more you stay inside, the less you want to go out. The more you decide to spend one more hour fixing these meaningless issues no one ever will care about, your life tears apart while everyone around you is having a coffee. There's no more coffee without work, now it's everywhere, even on holidays.

[Disconnection](https://assets.eurofound.europa.eu/f/279033/70e915b9f3/ef23002en.pdf) means risk of layoff. The market, oh, the market.

Is all of this worth it? Is all of this needed?

Can we all just be adults for a bit?
