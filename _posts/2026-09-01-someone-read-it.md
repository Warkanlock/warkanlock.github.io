I sent the PDF on a Tuesday after work, after renaming the file twice and staring at the title page long enough to start doubting the title, which is usually the point where I either rewrite the first scene or just submit the thing so I can stop touching it.

I submitted it.

Undefined Forest sat in a folder I would not show anyone, a short in Courier 12 with the usual superstitions about scene headings and whether the last line was doing any work. Software is honest about rejection: you compile, it fails, you know which line, you fix it. A screenplay just leaves. You don't get a stack trace from a festival. You get silence, and then an email that treats "official selection" like a type you could inspect.

It is not a type.

[Cal Film Festival](https://calfilmfestival.com) picked it for 2026. Los Angeles, shorts and scripts, a curated online festival, the kind of place that exists so a piece of writing can stop being a file on your machine. I am not going to dress this up as Cannes. I am also not going to pretend a stranger reading fifteen pages and saying yes is the same as nothing.

The bio on my sites has always been some version of "builds software and writes about systems," which is true, and which is also a convenient way of not saying I write screenplays, because that sentence looks unserious next to compilers and a job that already takes the whole day.

I wrote a short. Someone read it. They put it in.
